**Question: What does this mean?
Standardizing normally distributed data makes it easier to compare different sets of data.**

**Answer:**
It means converting different datasets into a common scale makes them easier to compare.

This process is called standardization. It converts data into Z-scores.

For example:

* Student A scores 80 in a test where the average is 70.

* Student B scores 90 in a test where the average is 80.

Although Student B has a higher score, we need to consider the average and standard deviation of each test to know who performed better relative to their classmates.

In simple words: Standardization helps us compare two different datasets fairly, even when they have different averages and standard deviations.

Remember: A Z-score tells us how many standard deviations a value is above or below the mean.

Let's continue with our previous example and standardize both students' scores.

### Step 1: Original data

|                    | Student A | Student B |
| ------------------ | --------- | --------- |
| Actual score       | 80        | 90        |
| Class average      | 70        | 80        |
| Standard deviation | 10        | 5         |

### Step 2: Calculate Z-scores

Formula:

Z=\\frac{X-\\text{Mean}}{\\text{SD}}

Student A:

Z=\\frac{80-70}{10}=1

Student B:

Z=\\frac{90-80}{5}=2

### Step 3: After standardization

|                        | Student A          | Student B          |
| ---------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------ |
| Original score         | 80                 | 90                 |
| Standardized score (Z) | +1                 | +2                 |
| Meaning                | 1 SD above average | 2 SD above average |

Visual comparison

### +1

Student B

### +2

2 standard deviations above average

### Final conclusion

Although Student B scored 90 and Student A scored 80, after standardization we discover that:

Student B performed better relative to their class.

That's why standardization is useful: it allows us to compare scores fairly even when the original datasets have different means and standard deviations.
